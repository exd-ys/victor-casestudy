Overview

The platform was right.
The design wasn't.

Viictor is a competitive tennis platform where amateur players enter level-based tournaments, issue Head 2 Head challenges, track their ranking, and win prize money. The mobile app had the right data model and the wrong face: a dark-background neon-green aesthetic that looked like every other amateur sports app, a visual system that contradicted itself screen to screen, and three primary navigation destinations that showed blank white space when empty.

The Problem

Technical Audit — Critical

The app was built without a design system. A developer working screen by screen produced a product where some screens had black header bars with white text, others had white headers with dark text, and forms used whichever style happened to be applied first. The effect was jarring — the app felt like two different products stitched together.

  • P0 Three primary nav screens blank — no empty states
  • P0 Dashboard has no primary action — zero forward momentum
  • P1 Header inconsistency across all screens — catastrophic split
  • P1 Players list shows names only — useless as a matchmaking tool
  • P1 Leaderboard treats rank 1 and rank 5 identically — emotionally inert
"6 of 8 cognitive load checklist items failed. The app consistently required users to hold information in working memory that the UI should have been providing."
Who Uses This
The forcing question
"A 35-year-old weekend player in Perth stands on a suburban outdoor court at 2:30pm, racquet in one hand, phone in the other, checking if their match invite from last night was accepted — bright Australian sun overhead, slight adrenaline, one-handed, impatient."

This sentence forced a light theme. Outdoor, bright sun, maximum legibility. It forced thumb-zone anchored actions — one-handed, racquet in the other hand. The old dark interface was the exact wrong answer for this scene. Every major design decision in the redesign traces back to this single sentence.

Design Direction

Three decisions.
Everything else followed.

Decision 01 — Colour
Restrained: green ≤10% of any screen
The current app used green as wallpaper — neon on dark, everywhere, at all times. The redesign treats green as a precision instrument. When it appears, it means something. A pending challenge, an active state, a earned moment. The moment it covers more than 10% of a screen, it becomes the template we escaped.
The Rarity Rule: scarcity is the signal.
Decision 02 — Theme
Light surfaces throughout
The scene sentence made this inevitable — outdoor, bright Australian sun, maximum legibility. The three anchor references (Strava, Nike Run Club, Apple Fitness+) all use light primary surfaces. The dark treatment was the category reflex, not a considered decision. Breaking from it was the biggest single visual statement of the rebrand.
Reference apps: Strava · Nike Run Club · Apple Fitness+
Decision 03 — Navigation
Side drawer → bottom tab bar
The hamburger menu in the top-left corner is the hardest tap target on a modern phone. It hides navigation behind an extra tap. It left the thumb zone — the bottom 40% of the screen — entirely empty on every single screen. Replacing it with a persistent bottom tab bar (Home · Play · Players · Rank · Me) fixed two P1 audit findings simultaneously.
Thumb zone rule: primary actions at the bottom, always.

Honest Uncertainty

What we'd test with real players.

These aren't gaps in the design — they're decisions that can only be validated by watching actual players use the app. The design system makes it easy to adjust any of them without rebuilding from scratch.

01
Does the large green ranking number feel motivating or discouraging for lower-ranked players?
The ranking hero is the emotional core of the dashboard. If #912 makes a player feel defeated rather than driven, we need a different treatment for that emotional state.
02
Do players find the "What's next" card sufficient, or do they want a full activity feed?
Surfacing the single most urgent action (pending challenge, closing tournament) was a deliberate simplification. It may be too sparse for active players with multiple things pending simultaneously.
03
Does "Head 2 Head / Tournaments" match players' mental model, or does the tab grouping cause confusion?
The five-tab bar (Home · Play · Players · Rank · Me) grouped competition-related features under "Play." Whether players naturally think in those categories is an assumption that needs validation.
What's Next

13 screens remaining.
The system is ready for them.

The redesign established a complete design system (DESIGN.md, PRODUCT.md, brand toolkit) before building a single screen. Each remaining screen inherits the tokens, rules, and patterns already defined — they're not starting from scratch.

Priority — build next
The Leaderboard
The emotional core of the platform. The screen that answers: "where do I stand against everyone else?" It needs top-3 distinct treatment, a user's own row pinned and highlighted, and a gap-to-next-rank delta that turns a sorted list into a competitive motivator. This is the screen where the dark "earned treatment" exception most clearly belongs.
Success metrics
How we'd know it worked
  • Daily active sessions increase (players returning to check challenges)
  • Tournament entry conversion rate from app (currently unmeasured)
  • Time-to-first-tournament for new users
  • Challenge acceptance rate after surfacing on dashboard
