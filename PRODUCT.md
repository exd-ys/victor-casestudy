## Users

The target audience for Viictor is competitive amateur tennis players (adults and juniors) who want flexible, self-organised matches with real stakes (entry fees, prize money), plus reputation and rating attached.

### Primary target audience

Amateur club and recreational players who already play socially but want more structured competition through monthly knockouts, box leagues, and Head 2 Head challenges.

Players comfortable paying entry fees and competing for prize money or vouchers, and who care enough about results to report scores properly and on time.

Adults who can book and pay for courts, bring balls, and arrange logistics themselves, preferring flexible scheduling over fixed club-organised league nights.

### Secondary audience segments

Juniors (and their parents or coaches) looking for extra match play outside official tournaments, with emphasis on experience and non-cash rewards.

"Serious social" players who want rankings, star ratings, and reputational signals (5-star rating, red flags, disputes) to matter in their local tennis community.

Players active on social media and email, who respond well to newsletters, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn presence, and ongoing updates.

### Positioning notes

Core value: "Play on your own schedule, against real opponents, for real stakes, while building a trusted competitive profile and ranking."

Core pain points addressed: lack of flexible but meaningful competition, no simple way to play for prize money locally, and no central record of conduct (ratings, red flags, disputes).

## Product Purpose

Viictor is a competitive tennis platform where players enter level-based tournaments and Head 2 Head challenges, organise their own matches, and win prize money. This use case focuses on redesigning the existing Home page and Tournament page so the core competition experience is clearer, faster to act on, and more trustworthy.

Three formats: Monthly Knockout, Box Leagues (round robin), and Head 2 Head challenges (unlocked after earning a verified Viictor rating). Prize money is paid via PayPal. Players have profiles with rankings, ratings, match history, and earnings. The platform is self-managed, no central venue, players coordinate home and away court logistics.

Success looks like: players returning for every new tournament cycle, challenging each other frequently, reporting results on time, and feeling proud to show the app to a fellow player.

## Brand Personality

Social, sporty, slick. The voice is confident without being aggressive — it's the app equivalent of a well-run tennis club: competitive, welcoming, and polished. Not a startup screaming for attention. Not a corporate scheduling tool. A platform that takes the sport seriously.

Three words: **Competitive. Connected. Premium.**

Emotional goals: players should feel capable and respected. The app should make them feel like their ranking matters, their matches are real, and their winnings are earned.

## Anti-references

- **Generic sports template** — dark background + neon green screaming from every element. The current Viictor app is the anti-reference. Green stays as a brand color, but used with restraint and purpose, not as wallpaper.
- **Corporate enterprise app** — cold, grey, lifeless. Reads like a HR scheduling tool, not a competitive sports platform.
- **Overly gamified** — badge soup, XP bars, confetti explosions. Treats the user like a child. Real competition doesn't need fake rewards on top of real ones.
- **Cluttered & busy** — information piled without hierarchy. Players on the court need instant legibility, not a wall of data to parse.

## Design Principles

1. **Restrained energy.** Sporty without screaming. Green is a precise tool, not decoration. One accent does more than five.
2. **Earned premium.** Every screen should feel like an app a serious player is proud to have on their phone. Typography, spacing, and hierarchy signal quality before any content loads.
3. **Social first, competitive second.** Competition is the hook, community keeps players. Design surfaces the social layer (challenges, messages, players nearby) before drowning it in statistics.
4. **Clarity under pressure.** Players check the app between games, on the court, in bright sunlight. Information must be instantly legible — no hunting, no cognitive load, no ambiguity about what to do next.
5. **One coherent system.** The current app's random split between light and dark screens is the single biggest trust-breaker. Every screen belongs to the same visual language.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum across all screens. Particular attention to:
- Contrast ratios on dark backgrounds (green-on-dark is a common AA failure point)
- Touch target sizes (minimum 44x44px — players may be using the app with sweaty hands)
- Text legibility in outdoor/bright light conditions
- Inclusive gender and disability categories already exist in the platform — reflect them without othering (no special "wheelchair mode" segregation in the UI)
